# Offers lens
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Easy-to-reach market.** Dalmatian tourism businesses are clustered, visible and can be met in person. A founder based in Omiš can walk in the door, and most competitors cannot.
- **Real, repeated pain.** Owners answer the same Wi-Fi, check-in, parking and price questions in four or more languages, often while they are driving, cooking or out on the water. The dream outcome is clear: guests get answers and booking requests are captured while the owner works.
- **Time and effort are already low.** The service is built in 1–2 weeks, the client tries it before paying the monthly fee, there is no long contract and the fee pauses off-season. On the time and effort side of the value equation this is already well designed.
- **The demo shows the result.** A live chat in German, Polish and Italian lets an owner see the result before buying anything.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Weak proof, so low likelihood.** There is one reference client, and it may be a related or family business. A non-technical owner has no reason yet to believe that "AI answers my guests" won't embarrass them with a wrong price or a missed booking. If nothing changes, this is where the offer fails.
2. **A thin, seasonal market with small budgets.** Many buyers are single-property owners with roughly five months of revenue. At €35/month with a pause off-season, each client may bring in only a few hundred euros a year. Whether enough clients exist to make a living is an open question: the number of target businesses, the close rate and the founder's hours are all unknown.
3. **A menu that is cheap and spread thin.** The studio sells five separate services, each with an "from" price. Low starting prices with no guarantee and no stack read as a commodity. It also invites comparison with free options, such as booking-platform auto-messages, the WhatsApp Business quick replies an owner sets up alone, or a nephew who builds websites.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Did Taxi Omiš Almissa pay, and can you show a number that changed for them, such as hours saved, bookings captured or reviews gained?
- How many target businesses are within a one-hour drive, and how many have you pitched so far? How many said yes?
- What does a client earn from one extra booking or one hour saved in August? Could you charge against that instead of against your costs?
- What happens when the assistant gets something wrong, and who carries that risk?
- How many hours a week can you give this, and how many clients can you support in peak season at once?
- Could you double the price of a bundled package and still be the obvious choice?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Get proof before scaling.** Sign 3–5 paying clients who are not related to you, measure before and after (questions handled, bookings captured, new reviews) and turn the results into case studies.
2. **Lead with one bundled offer.** For example, guest assistant, WhatsApp booking and reviews sold as a single package under a clear name, priced on the value of the outcome rather than on cost.
3. **Add a real guarantee.** For example: "If it doesn't capture X bookings or save you Y hours in your first month, you don't pay the setup fee." The guarantee must be checked with the CFO.
4. **Use real urgency.** Pre-season setup slots are limited by your build capacity. Sell them in Feb–April so every client is live before May.
5. **Size the market.** Count the target businesses and estimate the yearly revenue per client after off-season pauses before committing more time.
