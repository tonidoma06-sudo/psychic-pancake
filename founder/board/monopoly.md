# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **There is a small market that could be owned.** Owners of tourism businesses in Omiš and the Makarska Riviera are a small, defined group. They are not technical, they get the same questions from foreign guests over and over, and a founder who lives in Omiš can meet them in person. A remote agency or a global software tool cannot easily do the same. This is the right place to start: a large share of a small market.
- **Distribution is part of the design.** The founder meets clients in person, has a live demo chat in German, Polish and Italian, and lets clients try the product before paying the monthly fee. That is a real way to reach buyers, not just a product waiting to be found.
- **Monthly fees that can be paused off-season** keep a seasonal customer from cancelling for good. Over time, a base of retained, subscribed clients could become a local brand.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **There is no proprietary edge, so a copycat can appear in month six.** The founder builds with off-the-shelf AI tools. Any freelancer, web agency or booking platform could offer a similar guest assistant. Nothing here is ten times better. At best it is a small step forward on a product that is fast becoming a commodity. Without a moat, the result is a price war over thin margins.
2. **The founder sells five unrelated products.** Assistants, WhatsApp bots, automations, websites and review tools spread one person's time across several crowded categories. That makes the business a generalist agency instead of the owner of one niche. Agencies do not compound and do not scale beyond the founder's hours.
3. **Distribution is unproven.** There is one reference client, and it may be a family business that did not pay. Nobody knows how many prospects have been contacted or what each customer costs to win. The selling season is short, and only one person is doing the selling.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the secret? What do you know about Dalmatian hosts that a Split agency or a booking platform does not?
- Who else within reach of these owners already sells guest messaging, websites or review tools, and at what price? (Competitor pricing is unknown.)
- How many target businesses are in Omiš and on the Makarska Riviera, and what share could you hold within two seasons?
- Did Taxi Omiš Almissa pay, and is it a related business?
- How did your first paying customers arrive, and what did each one cost to win?
- What would make a client unable to leave in year three: their data, their integrations, the guest-review history?
- How many hours a week do you have, and what is your starting budget?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Narrow to one product for one segment**, such as the AI guest assistant for apartment and villa owners in Omiš, and set aside the other four.
2. **Prove distribution:** 10 paying, unrelated clients before the next peak season, with what each one cost to win written down.
3. **Name the moat being built:** a local network of host referrals, data on what guests ask that compounds across properties, or a recognised local brand. Show it getting stronger as the number of clients grows.
4. **Map the competitors** and their prices before setting the price.
