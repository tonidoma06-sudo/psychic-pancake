## The idea

- What it is: a one-person AI and digital services studio in Omiš, Croatia, that builds AI guest assistants, WhatsApp booking assistants, automations, websites and Google-review systems for small local businesses.
- Who it is for: small tourism-driven businesses in Dalmatia (Omiš, Split, Makarska Riviera): apartment and villa owners, taxi and transfer firms, tour/rafting/boat operators, restaurants; secondarily clinics and small trades. Owners who are not technical and get many repeated questions from foreign guests (German, Polish, Italian, English).
- What it sells, at what price (starting prices on the site):
  - AI guest assistant (QR code / link answering Wi-Fi, check-in, parking, local tips in the guest's language): from €35/month + €150 one-time setup per property.
  - WhatsApp booking assistant (answers, quotes prices, collects booking details, sends owner a summary to confirm): from €60/month + €300 setup.
  - Automations (morning booking summary, reminders, records/reports for the accountant): from €150 one-time.
  - Multilingual websites: from €400 (maintenance by agreement).
  - More Google reviews (post-visit review-request message, QR code, suggested replies): from €20/month + €100 setup.
  - WhatsApp and AI usage costs included in the monthly fee up to an agreed message volume. No long contract; monthly fee can be paused off-season. Built in 1–2 weeks; client tries it before paying the monthly fee.
- Where and how: based in Omiš; meets clients in person in Split, Omiš and the Makarska Riviera. Bilingual (HR/EN) website with a free-call contact form that sends to WhatsApp. First reference client: Taxi Omiš Almissa (family taxi/transfer business), for which a 6-language website and a ride log (rides, fuel, profit per vehicle, driver commission) were built.
- Budget and constraints: not stated. Founder's contact details are not yet on the website. Founder builds with AI tools (Claude). Seasonal market (tourism peaks roughly May–October).

## Summary

**Verdict: Not yet.** Two checks fail: year 1 makes a loss and too few simulated buyers buy. **What has to change:** lead with the WhatsApp booking assistant for taxi, transfer and tour operators at €79/month, and sell the guest assistant only to hosts with 3 or more properties and to villa owners. Prove both with paid pilots before launch (`launch.md`). Then re-run `/founder-consumer` on operators and `/founder-plan`.

**What it is.** Almissa Digital is a one-person studio in Omiš that sets up AI guest assistants, WhatsApp booking assistants, review tools and websites for small tourism businesses on the Omiš–Makarska coast. It is set up in person and supported locally.

**The three numbers** (`cfo.md`, guest assistant at €120 per season ≈ €20 per property-month):
- Contribution **€16.90 per property-month (84%)**. The unit is healthy.
- Break-even is **26 active properties every month**, because about €431/month of fixed costs keep running in winter. €290.98 of that is the 2026 paušalni obrt contribution.
- Year 1: **−€3,397** at the panel-supported ramp (peaking at 20 properties). For the whole business, worked out by hand (guest assistant + 8 WhatsApp operators with setup fees + 4 websites), it's about **+€1,160 before paying the founder anything**.

**Where the board and the panel agreed:** the business is spread across too many products. Trust is the barrier (a one-person company, and the risk of a wrong answer turning into a bad review). Proof from a host people know matters more than price. **Where they disagreed:** the Offers lens wanted a bigger bundle at a higher price; the Monopoly and Product lenses wanted one narrow product. The panel sided with narrowing. Price objections almost disappeared at €20/month, yet the buy rate stayed at 5%.

**Biggest risk:** for most apartment hosts there is no real need. The free Booking/Airbnb messages plus "I live next door" cover it (0 of 24 single-apartment hosts bought). **What to do about it:** stop targeting single-apartment hosts, sell to operators (the pre-booking gap no platform covers) and to multi-property hosts, and build social proof with 5 Pioneer installs.

**What the founder needs to start:** about **€700** in startup costs, plus a cash cushion of about **€2,000–€4,000** to cover winter fixed costs (`cfo.md`). Or much less if this runs as a second activity next to a job, where contributions are assessed yearly. That's for an accountant to confirm.

**The one thing to do this week:** book an accountant meeting about the obrt type (main vs second activity). That single answer moves year 1 by up to €3,490. Also put your contact details on the website.

### 5. The gap

The gap is **done-for-you setup, local and in person, with a monthly WhatsApp assistant that handles enquiries before anyone has booked.** The evidence:

- No Split, Omiš or Makarska provider of AI or WhatsApp assistants appeared in the searches. The nearest is a Pula freelancer selling a one-off build.
- Free native tools only reach guests who have already booked. Booking.com's Auto-Reply "mostly reach[es] a guest who has already booked" ([Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4135090/read-before-the-guest-bookingcoms-ai-answers-before-you-can)). Taxi, transfer, tour and boat operators get direct WhatsApp enquiries that no OTA tool covers.
- The top complaints (unreachable support, billing traps, setup effort, wrong AI answers) all point to what a local person who sets it up, monitors it and bills a flat fee that can be paused would fix.

**Where there is no gap:** a plain FAQ assistant for apartments that are already on Booking or Airbnb. Free built-in tools and $7–19 SaaS tools cover it, and Almissa's €35 is hard to defend there unless the done-for-you service is the product.

### 6. The threat

- **Rentlio is the fastest copier.** It is Croatian, already sells AI guest communication from about €12 per month, and already has Croatian host relationships and integrations ([Chekin](https://chekin.com/en/blog/chekin-rentlio-integration/)). It could add a WhatsApp channel and outbound sales in the Split region within a season.
- **Booking.com and Airbnb** keep making auto-replies free and smarter ([Travolution](https://www.travolution.com/news/booking.com-launches-agentic-ai-tools/)). This shrinks the apartment use case.
- **Local web freelancers and agencies** can copy Almissa's stack in weeks, since Almissa itself builds with off-the-shelf AI tools. The Pula Contra listing at $200 shows this is already happening.
- **Apartmanica** could bundle an AI assistant into its websites for Croatian hosts.

The defensible parts are local presence, reference clients and reviews, and being the one person a non-technical owner can phone.

## Pricing

All amounts are in euros. The tools print "$", but every input was in €.

### 1. The price

**€120 per property for the season (May–October), which works out to €20 a month. No setup fee if bought by 31 March. Each extra property is €60 a season.**

Why this price:
- **What buyers said** (`pricing-curve.md`, 40 simulated hosts): the acceptable range is **€8–€25 a month**, the point of least resistance is €10, and the point where as many hosts call it a bargain as call it expensive is €15. The current €35 + €150 setup is **above the range**. For the median host, €35 is exactly "too expensive, I would not buy."
- **What competitors charge** (`competitors.md`): self-serve guest assistant tools cost about $7–$29 per property per month, with a median of about $19. Rentlio, a Croatian property-management system, sells AI guest messaging from about €12. Airbnb and Booking.com auto-replies are free. At €20 a month, Almissa sits near the competitor median while doing the setup in person, which is the one thing nobody else does.
- **What the business needs** (CFO tool, the same costs at every price): €3.10 of variable cost per property-month and about €420 a month of fixed costs, which are estimates.

| price per property-month | contribution | break-even (active properties) | margin at 40 properties | year 1 (with the season ramp) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| €15 | €11.90 | 36 | 9% | −€3,005 |
| **€20** | **€16.90** | **25** | **32%** | **−€2,150** |
| €25 | €21.90 | 20 | 46% | −€1,295 |
| €35 | €31.90 | 14 | 61% | +€415 (but the panel says few will buy at €35) |

**The honest result:** no price inside the range buyers accept makes the guest assistant alone profitable in year 1. It runs at a loss through the winter months. The assistant has to be the way in that leads to higher-value work. That means the review booster, the WhatsApp booking assistant for taxi, tour and boat operators, and websites. The ladder below does that.

Each client needs its own WhatsApp API number, about **€25/month** through a provider such as 360dialog (third-party price sources: [360dialog docs](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/get-started/pricing), [ezcontact.ai](https://ezcontact.ai/en/blog/2026-07-04-360dialog-pricing-2026-real-monthly-costs-api-fees)), plus Meta's per-message fees ([Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)). At €60 a month, a client leaves only **€25.20** (42%), and break-even is 18 clients. **At €79 a month**, a client leaves €44.20 and break-even is 10. The monthly fees alone lose **−€3,912** in year 1 at €60. The tool can't model setup fees, which add 8 × €300 = **+€2,400**.
- **The line to watch: off-season fixed costs.** Volume −20% makes the guest assistant's year 1 **−€3,752**. A ±10% price change moves it only about €200.
- **Cash.** The guest assistant alone needs **about €4,097** before it pays for itself. Scenario B needs about €4,612, or about €2,200 once setup fees are counted.
- **The combined business** (one set of fixed costs, worked out by hand from the tool runs):
  guest assistant contribution €1,775 + WhatsApp contribution €1,260 + WhatsApp setups €2,400 + 4 websites × €400 = €1,600, minus fixed costs 12 × €431 = €5,172, minus startup costs €700 = **about +€1,160 in year 1, before paying the founder anything.**

### Three ways to improve the margin
1. **Check whether this can run as a second activity next to a job.** If so, paušalni obrt contributions are assessed yearly on income instead of €290.98 every month ([fiskai.hr](https://www.fiskai.hr/alati/kalkulator-doprinosa/)). That could be worth up to **+€3,490 a year**, more than every other lever combined. Confirm with an accountant.
2. **Raise the WhatsApp booking assistant to €79 a month**, or pass the €25 number fee through as a separate line. Contribution goes from €25.20 to **€44.20**, and break-even drops from 18 to **10 clients** (tool what-if).
3. **Charge €75 setup per guest-assistant property after 31 March**, and sell the "Better" rung (€180 a season). At 20 properties that adds about €1,500 + €300.

### 4. Routines (one page each)

**A. Setting up a new property**
1. Visit: take a photo of the house rules, Wi-Fi, parking, check-in method and nearby tips. Agree on what the assistant must **never** answer (prices, refunds, damage).
2. Write the answers in the table, in Croatian first.
3. The client approves every line, signed in WhatsApp ("Odobreno").
4. Generate the translations, then spot-check DE and PL with a native speaker or a second tool.
5. Test 20 questions, including 5 it should forward. All 5 must reach the client's WhatsApp.
6. Print and laminate the QR card with the host's name and WhatsApp number on it.
7. Sign the GDPR data-processing agreement.
8. Send the confirmation message (`brand.md`, touchpoint 3).

**B. A wrong answer or a complaint**
1. Reply to the client within 1 hour in season: thank them, say what happened.
2. Fix the answer and add it to "never answer, always forward" if it was risky.
3. Retest the same question in all languages.
4. Log it: date, question, wrong answer, fix.
5. Offer the 30-day refund if they're within the window. Don't argue.

**C. Weekly in season (Friday)**
1. Review the forward log: which questions forwarded most? Add answers, with approval.
2. Check uptime and errors.
3. Update the client count and the numbers in `launch.md`.
4. Send one "question of the week" post (anonymised, with permission).

**D. End of season (1 November)**
1. Send each client their "Your August in numbers" report.
2. Pause the assistants. Offer a winter mode for clients who rent all year.
3. March: send the renewal offer with the pre-season deadline.

### 6. Licences, permits and insurance (checklist)

- [ ] **Paušalni (lump-sum) taxation:** check eligibility and the revenue limit with the Tax Administration (https://porezna-uprava.gov.hr/en/obrtnici-pausalisti/4564). Contributions are about €290.98/month in 2026 if this is the main activity, or **yearly if it's a second activity next to a job** ([fiskai](https://www.fiskai.hr/alati/kalkulator-doprinosa/)).
- [ ] **OIB and a business bank account.**
- [ ] **Invoicing and fiscalisation rules** (including the e-invoicing obligations being rolled out in Croatia). Confirm with an accountant which apply to your obrt.
- [ ] **GDPR:** a data-processing agreement template (Almissa as processor, the host as controller), a record of processing, and the AI and WhatsApp providers as sub-processors with EU data handling where possible. Guidance: Croatia's data protection agency AZOP (https://azop.hr/).
- [ ] **AI transparency:** tell guests clearly that they are talking to an AI assistant (the EU AI Act transparency duties for chatbots). Put it on the QR card and as the first line of the chat.
- [ ] **Professional liability insurance:** optional, but worth a quote given the "wrong answer" risk.
