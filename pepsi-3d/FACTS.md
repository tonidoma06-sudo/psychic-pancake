# Facts in the film

| On screen / in the voice-over | Source |
|---|---|
| A Pepsi "Pepsi Stuff" TV ad (1995–96) ended with a teen landing a Harrier jet at school: "HARRIER FIGHTER 7,000,000 PEPSI POINTS" | Leonard v. PepsiCo, 88 F. Supp. 2d 116 (S.D.N.Y. 1999); Wikipedia |
| The rules let you buy Pepsi Points for 10¢ each (with at least 15 original points) | court opinion; Wikipedia; The Hustle |
| 7,000,000 × 10¢ = $700,000; John Leonard sent a check for $700,008.50 (incl. shipping) | court opinion ("a check for $700,008.50"); Wikipedia |
| He raised the money from investors (sources: five, incl. businessman Todd Hoffman) — the film just says "investors" | Wikipedia; Netflix "Pepsi, Where's My Jet?" (2022) |
| A Harrier was worth tens of millions (estimates $32M–$37.4M) — the film says "over thirty million" | The Hustle ($33.8M); Netflix Tudum ($32M); Wikipedia ($37.4M) |
| Pepsi refused ("fanciful", "humorous"); Leonard sued | Wikipedia; court opinion |
| Judge Kimba Wood ruled for Pepsi: "no objective person could reasonably have concluded that the commercial actually offered consumers a Harrier Jet" (affirmed, 2d Cir. 2000) — the film paraphrases: "no reasonable person" | court opinion; Netflix Tudum |
| Pepsi re-aired the ad with the jet at 700,000,000 points and "just kidding" | The Hustle; Mental Floss; Creative Bloq |

Simplified in the film: the court fight was a summary judgment (no trial); the courtroom scene stages it as a ruling.
