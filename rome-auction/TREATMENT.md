# "SOLD! The Day Rome Was Auctioned": Game Show Flat, YouTube Short

**Brief.** Story #14 from *Finance & Business Shorts: Fact-Checked Scripts* (verdict: TRUE). Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, about 35 s. English. **The user records the voice-over later.** Until then the film is timed to a scratch read (Kokoro TTS, a placeholder only), and the whole timeline is rebuilt from the user's audio with one command (`retime.py`: Whisper word timings → line starts → beat-snapped scenes → score regenerated). Captions are word-by-word telop plates timed to the voice. No storyboard stop.

**The script (word for word, the voice-over):**
1. In the year 193, the Roman Empire was sold… at an auction.
2. The palace guards had just killed the emperor.
3. Then they offered the throne to the highest bidder.
4. The emperor's father-in-law offered every soldier 20,000 coins.
5. A rich senator, Didius Julianus, bid 25,000.
6. He won, and the Senate made him emperor.
7. 66 days later, a rival general marched on Rome… and Julianus was executed.
8. He bought an entire empire, and kept it for just 66 days.

## Three candidate structures
1. **A game show auction in rounds.** Cold open on the judgement ("SOLD!"), then Round 1 *The Empty Throne*, Round 2 *Bidding War* (split stage, call-and-response paddles, counters racing), the "SOLD!" judgement, Round 3 *66 Days* (one pattern escalating: the calendar ticks quarters → eighths → sixteenths as the army marches in), and an ending report card that loops back to the gavel.
2. **A quiz show.** Three contestants guess "how long did he keep it?" while the story plays; the answer is revealed at the end. Fun, but it competes with the voice-over for attention.
3. **One long round.** A single price tag escalates the whole film (20,000 → 25,000 → the cost of 66 days). Tight, but too little variety for a 35 s Short whose job is to hold attention.

**Chosen: 1.** The auction *is* a game show: bids are call-and-response, prices are game-UI counters, "SOLD!" is a judgement word, and the 66-day countdown is the escalating pattern. It changes set and colour every 2–5 s (the Shorts rhythm), and the last beat cuts back to the first frame for a seamless loop.

## Logline and arc
In 193 AD the palace guards auctioned off the Roman Empire to the highest bidder, and the winner kept his prize for just 66 days.
Setup (an empty throne, a FOR SALE sign) → turn (the bidding war, SOLD!) → ending (the 66-day countdown runs out; the report card reads "25,000 per soldier · kept 66 days").

## Cast (one bean construction, told apart by colour, one accessory and a name tag)
- **Guards**: red beans with a crested helmet and a spear, tag GUARD (the crowd). Never graphic: the emperor's murder is told by an empty throne with a fallen laurel wreath.
- **Sulpicianus**: pink bean, a white toga sash, tag FATHER-IN-LAW, a bid paddle (a character is never the colour of its set: he stands on teal).
- **Didius Julianus**: gold-yellow bean (he is the rich one), a purple toga sash, tag SENATOR, a bid paddle. Gets the gold laurel crown.
- **Severus**: orange bean, a general's helmet, tag RIVAL GENERAL, leading a marching row of soldiers.
- **The Senate**: a hand holding a giant rubber stamp, EMPEROR.

## Beat sheet and cue map
120 bpm (0.5 s per beat, 2 s per bar). Every scene starts on a beat chosen from the voice: the beat at or just before the spoken start of its line. Key hits land on the word: the gavel on "sold", the paddles on "20,000" and "25,000", the stamp on "emperor", the calendar's last tick on "executed", the report-card stamp on "66 days". Without a voice, those words are placed from the scratch read.

Music: a bright major pop loop, one chord per bar (I–V–vi–IV in F), synthesized; a flavour per round: cold open = brass stab fanfare; Round 1 = funk slap bass; Round 2 = quiz (marimba + cowbell, tempo feel doubles in the counter); SOLD = crash + rising brass; Round 3 = drums only for one bar, then hats subdividing with the calendar; ending = the fanfare again, so the loop seam is musical.

## As built (changes after the look tests)
- Round cards became corner banners: at a voice-over pace, a 1-bar card would stop the story every few seconds.
- SOLD! lands on the hard cut itself (the beat of "he won"), and a 25,000 price tag hangs on the throne from then on, so the winning bid stays readable.
- Camera moves: beat punches throughout, the split stage (round 2), a snap zoom onto the 25,000 counter, a vertical drop into round 3, and hard cuts with a white flash.
- Marquee bulbs blink on the beat along the bottom of every set; the last beat replays the first frame so the Short loops seamlessly.
