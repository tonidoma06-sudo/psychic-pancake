# Facts in the film

| On screen / in the voice-over | Source |
|---|---|
| Second Lieutenant Hiroo Onoda was sent to Lubang Island in the Philippines in December 1944, with orders never to surrender (and not to take his own life) | Wikipedia, *Hiroo Onoda*; History Hit; Britannica |
| The war ended in August 1945. Leaflets saying so were dropped over the island (including a B-17 drop late in 1945 carrying General Yamashita's surrender order); Onoda and his men judged them fakes | Wikipedia; History Hit; History.co.uk |
| Newspapers were left for him, and letters and photographs from the soldiers' families were dropped from the air (1950s); he dismissed them as tricks | Wikipedia; History.co.uk |
| He kept up his guerrilla campaign for decades; he and his men killed about 30 Filipino villagers in raids (the count is approximate across sources, so the film says "about 30") | Wikipedia; History Hit; Explorersweb |
| In February 1974 Norio Suzuki, a young Japanese traveller, went looking for "Lieutenant Onoda, a panda, and the Abominable Snowman, in that order" (his own words to friends before he left; the film quotes them) | Wikipedia, *Norio Suzuki (explorer)*; Big Think; Today I Found Out |
| Suzuki found Onoda on Lubang after four days of searching (20 February 1974). Onoda refused to surrender without orders from his commanding officer | Wikipedia; nippon.com |
| The government found his former commander, Major Yoshimi Taniguchi, by then a bookseller, and flew him to Lubang | Wikipedia; nippon.com |
| On 9 March 1974 Taniguchi read him the order relieving him of duty, and Onoda surrendered, 29 years after the war ended (August 1945 to March 1974) | Wikipedia; nippon.com; Today in History |
| He handed over his sword, his working Type 99 rifle, about 500 rounds of ammunition and several hand grenades | Wikipedia; History.co.uk; rarehistoricalphotos.com |

The film is non-graphic: no violence is shown. The raids are told in one line over a quiet dusk shot of a farm, with the toll on screen,
and the score drops out under it.

Dramatisation: the cartoon characters are stylised stand-ins with no real likeness. The plane, the shop, the leaflet text (shown in
English for the viewer; the real leaflets were in Japanese), the checklist and the "STILL WORKS" gag are illustrations of the facts above.
The film shows the 1974 meeting at Onoda's own clearing; the scenes are compressed.
