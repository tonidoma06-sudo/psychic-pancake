# Facts in the film

| On screen / in the voice-over | Source |
|---|---|
| In 1999 a Healthy Choice promotion gave frequent-flyer miles for UPC barcodes mailed in (the film says "a pudding brand"; the brand on screen is invented) | Wikipedia, *David Phillips (entrepreneur)*; UC Davis news |
| David Phillips, a civil engineer at UC Davis, worked out the miles were worth more than the food | UC Davis news; Gizmodo |
| He bought 12,150 cups of chocolate pudding, for a little over $3,000 | Wikipedia; UC Davis ($3,140 of pudding and other Healthy Choice products) |
| With the deadline close, he donated the pudding to the Salvation Army, whose volunteers peeled the labels for him (the film says "charity" / "food bank") | Wikipedia; UC Davis |
| He earned about 1.25 million miles (1,253,000) and lifetime elite (AAdvantage Gold) status | Wikipedia; Supercar Blondie / FlyerTalk reports |
| The donation gave him a tax deduction (about $815) | Wikipedia; Gadling |

Rounded in the voice-over: "twelve thousand cups" (12,150), "over a million miles" (1.25 million).
