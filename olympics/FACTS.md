# Facts in the film

| On screen / in the voice-over | Source |
|---|---|
| The men's marathon of the 1904 St. Louis Olympics, run on 30 August 1904; the Games were held alongside the World's Fair | Wikipedia, *Athletics at the 1904 Summer Olympics – Men's marathon*; Olympedia |
| About 90 °F, a course of roughly 25 miles (24.85 mi / 40 km) on dusty country roads with hills; officials' cars ahead raised dust | Olympedia; Wikipedia; Smithsonian Magazine |
| 32 runners started; many were inexperienced | Wikipedia, *Frederick Lorz*; Olympedia |
| Water was scarce. Sources disagree on the number of water stops (one well at mile 12, or two stops), so the film says "barely any". The organizer, James E. Sullivan, was interested in "purposeful dehydration" | Olympedia; St. Louis County Library guide; popular accounts |
| Fred Lorz dropped out with cramps around mile 9 and rode in a car until it broke down near mile 19 (about 10–11 miles), then ran into the stadium and was greeted as the winner; he was photographed with Alice Roosevelt, the President's daughter, and was about to receive the medal when the ride was revealed | Wikipedia; Olympedia; Explore the Archive |
| Lorz said it was a joke; the AAU banned him for life, lifted the ban after he apologized, and he won the 1905 Boston Marathon | ABC News; Olympics.com; Olympedia |
| Thomas Hicks was given strychnine (a rat poison that is a stimulant in small doses) in egg white, then a second dose with brandy; he hallucinated, thought the finish was 20 miles away, and was held up by his trainers across the line | Wikipedia, *Thomas Hicks (athlete)*; Olympedia; RunBlogRun |
| Hicks won in 3:28:53, the slowest winning time in Olympic marathon history (by about half an hour), and lost 8 pounds during the race | Wikipedia; Olympedia |
| Félix Carvajal, a Havana mailman, lost his money gambling in New Orleans and hitchhiked to St. Louis; he ran in trousers cut down into shorts by another athlete; he chatted with spectators, ate green apples, got cramps, lay down (the nap is in popular accounts), and finished fourth | Olympedia; Wikipedia, *Andarín Carvajal*; Britannica blog; Today I Found Out |
| Len Taunyane, a Tswana runner from South Africa and one of the first Black Africans at the Olympics, was chased about a mile off course by a dog and finished ninth | Wikipedia; Olympedia |
| More than half the field did not finish (18 of 32 in one account); William Garcia came close to death | Wikipedia; Olympedia |

Simplifications: the cars, the stadium, the World's Fair and the people are stylised toon stand-ins with no likeness; bib
numbers are decorative. The order of some mid-race events is compressed. "Rat poison" is how strychnine is commonly described;
the film shows no harm beyond the historical record.
