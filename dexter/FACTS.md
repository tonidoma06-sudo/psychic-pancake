# Facts in the film

| On screen / in the voice-over | Source |
|---|---|
| Timothy Dexter (1747–1806) was a merchant of Newburyport, Massachusetts, famous for his eccentricity and his luck; he styled himself "Lord" Timothy Dexter | Britannica, *Timothy Dexter*; Wikipedia, *Timothy Dexter* |
| Rival merchants reportedly gave him deliberately bad advice, hoping to ruin him | Wikipedia; New England Historical Society |
| He shipped warming pans (bed warmers) to the West Indies, where they were sold to the molasses industry as ladles | Wikipedia; New England Historical Society |
| He sent wool mittens to the West Indies; they were bought by traders who shipped them on to Siberia | Wikipedia; New England Historical Society |
| Told to send coal to Newcastle (England's coal capital), his shipment arrived during a miners' strike and sold at a profit | Wikipedia; New England Historical Society |
| In 1802 he wrote *A Pickle for the Knowing Ones*, with eccentric spelling and almost no punctuation | Wikipedia, *A Pickle for the Knowing Ones*; Project Gutenberg edition |
| The second edition added a page of punctuation marks with his note that readers could "peper and solt it as they plese" | Wikipedia, *A Pickle for the Knowing Ones*; Public Domain Review |

Most of these trade stories come from Dexter's own boasts and later retellings: historians treat the details (and the order)
as uncertain, so the film tells them as the story is usually told. "1790s" is approximate. The page text shown in the study is a
pastiche in Dexter's style, not a quotation; the punctuation-page note is quoted from the book. The Newburyport, Caribbean,
Siberian and Newcastle settings are stylised, and all characters are toon stand-ins with no real likeness.
