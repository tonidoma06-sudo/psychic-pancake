# Facts in the film

| On screen / in the voice-over | Source |
|---|---|
| In 1964 Reg Spiers, an Australian javelin thrower, was stuck in London and broke (an injury had ended his Tokyo Olympics hopes) | BBC; Wikipedia, *Human mail* |
| He wanted to be home for his daughter's birthday | BBC; inkl / Australian press |
| A friend (John McSorley) built the crate and helped ship him as air cargo, cash on delivery | BBC; Atlas Obscura |
| Inside: a pillow, a flashlight (torch), tins of food, and bottles (one for water, one to pee in) | BBC |
| About 63 hours later (after delays, including a hot stopover), the crate landed in Perth; he got out and walked out of the airport | BBC; Wikipedia; Atlas Obscura (hour counts vary slightly between accounts) |

On screen, invented for the film: the faces and clothes, the bedsit, the London street, the field, the shed, the airline livery,
the stencils on the crate, Perth airport's look, the birthday room. Reg's daughter is not named.
