# Facts in the film

| On screen / in the voice-over | Source |
|---|---|
| In 1987 Steven Rothstein, a Chicago-area businessman, bought a lifetime unlimited first-class pass from an airline (American Airlines' "AAirpass") for about $250,000 (reported as $233,509.93 after a credit) | Los Angeles Times, Ken Bensinger, May 2012; Wikipedia, *AAirpass* |
| Two years later he paid about $150,000 more for a companion add-on: a second seat for anyone he chose | Los Angeles Times 2012 |
| He flew more than 10,000 flights on the pass | Los Angeles Times 2012 (millions of miles, 10,000+ flights) |
| He flew to London again and again on a whim, and often gave the companion seat to strangers he met | Los Angeles Times 2012 |
| The airline later decided such passes were costing it heavily; "by some estimates, millions" (figures in reports vary; none stated on screen beyond "$ MILLIONS") | Los Angeles Times 2012 |
| In December 2008, at the gate for a flight to London (Chicago), he was told his pass was revoked | Los Angeles Times 2012 |
| The airline accused him of fraud, of booking seats under made-up names that went unused (thousands of reservations that were no-shows) | Los Angeles Times 2012 |
| He sued for about $7 million (the film doesn't state the outcome, which is reported inconsistently) | Los Angeles Times 2012; Chicago Tribune |

On screen, invented for the film: the airline's name and look ("SKYLINE", a generic brand), the ticket counter, the boardroom and its chart, the gate agent, the scissors, the empty cabin with "RESERVED" tags, the courtroom, the companions' faces, the boarding pass, the flight times on the board.
The narration says "the airline"; the film shows no real airline's name or logo.
