# Facts in the film

| On screen / in the voice-over | Source |
|---|---|
| In late 1932 the Australian army was sent against emus in Western Australia ("the Emu War"): about 20,000 emus were eating and trampling the wheat in the Campion district | Wikipedia, *Emu War*; Britannica, *Emu War* |
| The force: Major G. P. W. Meredith of the Royal Australian Artillery, two soldiers, two Lewis machine guns and 10,000 rounds | Wikipedia; History Hit |
| An ambush at a dam: the birds split into small groups and ran; a gun jammed | Wikipedia; History Hit; Modern Farmer |
| A gun was mounted on a truck; the emus outran it and the ride was too rough to aim (the film shows "50 km/h", the emu's top speed) | Wikipedia; Britannica |
| The army withdrew on 8 November 1932, about a week in ("within a week, the army retreated"; the film leaves out the short second attempt later that month) | Wikipedia |
| Meredith is credited with saying that a division with the birds' "bullet-carrying capacity" could face any army in the world. The wording comes from a 1953 newspaper retrospective, so the film says "the Major said" and paraphrases | Wikipedia; Modern Farmer |
| Farmers asked for military help again in 1934, 1943 and 1948; the requests were refused | Wikipedia |

The film is non-graphic: no birds are hurt on screen (the emus dodge, scatter, outrun the truck and "win").
