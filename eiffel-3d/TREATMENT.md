# "The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower": 3D Short (own style: Toy Theatre 3D, Paris 1925)

Brief: vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, about 44 s, English, the creator's own voice-over later (timed to a scratch read until
then; `build.sh your-voice.wav` re-times everything). Asked for: better-looking characters than the Rome film, better
movement, more background detail, quieter music.

**What changed from the Rome film**
- Characters: one sculpted head mesh per person (skull, brow, sockets, cheekbones, nose with wings, jaw, chin, hair and
  moustache volume) with the face painted on it: coloured irises with highlights, lids that blink and wink, brows that act,
  a mouth that talks; ears, glasses, hats that sit on the head. Bodies wear tailored 1920s suits painted onto the torso
  (pinstripe, tweed with a waistcoat and watch chain, lapels, ties, pocket squares), padded shoulders, open sleeves with
  cuffs, jointed fingers, a high collar.
- Movement: eased pose tracks with a little overshoot, breathing, eye and head look-at, blinks and winks, walk and run
  cycles locked to the distance travelled, reading, stamping, driving, waving, hiding the face.
- Backgrounds: the Eiffel Tower in lattice, a Haussmann boulevard (shops, awnings, balconies, mansard roofs, trees, lamps,
  a Morris column, a café), a grand hotel salon (boiserie, chandelier, fireplace, portraits), the Hôtel de Crillon front
  (colonnade, arcade, flags, canopy) with a police station across the street, an open 1920s tourer.
- Music: a swing-musette caper (accordion, pompe guitar, walking bass, brushes), 14 dB under the voice (Rome: 11 dB).
