# Facts in the film

Victor Lustig's Eiffel Tower con, as told by the sources below. Where the sources disagree (the exact sums, how long he
waited before the second attempt), the film says nothing specific.

| On screen / in the voice-over | Source and wording |
|---|---|
| 1925: Lustig "sold" the Eiffel Tower to a scrap-metal dealer | Wikipedia, *Victor Lustig* · Smithsonian Magazine, *The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice.* |
| He read a newspaper story about how costly the tower was to maintain and repaint | same; Mental Floss |
| He posed as a government official (deputy director-general of the Ministère des Postes et Télégraphes), on forged stationery | same |
| He invited scrap dealers to a confidential meeting at the Hôtel de Crillon | same; Bonjour Paris |
| He told them the city would sell the tower for scrap and that the deal had to stay secret | same |
| André Poisson paid, and also bribed him to secure the deal | same |
| Lustig left with the money; Poisson was too embarrassed to go to the police | same |
| He came back and tried the same con again; this time a dealer went to the police and Lustig fled before arrest | same (the timing of the second attempt varies by source, so the film gives none) |

Not claimed: amounts (the sources disagree), "sold it twice" (the second sale did not go through).
The lesson card ("secret, urgent, too good to miss = scam") is advice, not a historical claim.
